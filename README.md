# Project 1: *Manuscript*

Demo/template for our [first projects](https://typography-interaction-2627.github.io/project/1/).

> **Students will choose a seminal design text from [readings.design](https://readings.design), read and respond to it, and typeset their selection and reply together.**
>
> The goal of this project is to hone your basic skills in typography, focusing on expression, hierarchy, and form appropriate to a work. You will do this through exploration, trial and error, and responding to critical feedback. And then you will execute this typesetting in code, as a web page

# About the project

I chose Aggie Toppins’s article, “Can We Teach Graphic Design History Without Chronology?” She questions the idea that design history is a timeline where each period is seen as an improvement on the one before it. That made me think about other ways to organize history.

In my response, I argue that we can understand design history across different dimensions, rather than seeing it as a straight line of progress. For example, we can look at its cultural and social context, not just what was produced during a particular period.

# How I Made It

When building this website, I used HTML elements such as header, body, section, and footer to organize the selected reading and my response. I also tried to create a clearer visual hierarchy for quotes and important paragraphs. I then moved on to CSS to adjust the typography, colors, spacing, and layout. During this process, I moved back and forth between HTML and CSS, adjusting how I grouped elements and used classes and variables to achieve the result I was looking for. I spent quite a lot of time organizing the different levels of content into sections. I also spent some time experimenting with the quote styling to see what I could achieve.
